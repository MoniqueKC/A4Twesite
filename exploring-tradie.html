The Kaizen ladder

A process business is built one rung at a time. There is no other way up.

You have heard the pitch. Fifteen minutes of marketing a day, ten times the business, you on a beach. You already know it is not true. What nobody tells you is what the real work actually looks like.

It looks like this. A business you can step back from is a process business: many small steps, each one standardised, all lined up under one goal. A ladder. You do not jump to the top and you do not need to. You climb one rung, stand on it until it holds, then the next.

That is what Kaizen is for. It names the rungs and it gives you the order. My job is to name the problem with you, help you see what you are climbing for and then break the climb into steps you can actually take between jobs. Steps, decisions and a framework to make them with. Nothing else works and I will not tell you otherwise.

Glossary

The words first. Without the words you cannot have the thinking.

These are the words I use in sessions. Once you have them, you can see the problem and then you can change it.

WasteAnything in the job the customer would not pay for if they saw it. Eight kinds: waiting, transport, motion, over-processing, defects, inventory, over-production, unused talent.
BottleneckThe one point everything queues behind. Often the owner. Find it first; fixing anything else is wasted.
FrustrationYour early-warning system. Every frustration marks friction in the workflow that can be looked at and fixed.
FrictionAnything that causes delay, confusion or rework. Some friction is useful — a check before you pay. The sweet spot is just enough to catch mistakes, not enough to slow the work.
KaizenJapanese for continuous improvement, and the movement that changed manufacturing — "The Toyota Way". Guided by a North Star. Focused on zen workflows and engagement.
5SSort, set in order, shine, standardise, sustain. A place for everything — the ute, the yard, the software.
Five whysAsk why five times to get past the symptom to the cause you can change.
PDCAPlan, do, check, act. The small loop that keeps an improvement true.
Back-costingReading a finished job backwards: what it cost, what it earned, where the margin came from. Best against the quote.
Clean dataInformation organised, current and reliable enough to make a decision from. Jobs closed, quotes attached, bills on the right section.
SimplicityNot the absence of complexity but the other side of it. Work through the detail, bring lean to it, strip the waste, know the value. The result feels simple.
FlowWork moving from enquiry to quote to job to invoice to cash with as few interruptions as possible.
Flow stateWhen the work feels effortless — the right amount of friction and ease — and time flies. In sport it is peak performance. Aim to work in flow state for part of every day.
Zen workCalm, one thing at a time, finished. Reached step by step — never all at once.
North StarThe one thing the business is aiming at, so the decisions in between get easier.
EngagementStaff involved and caring. Purpose beyond profit, clear workflows, clear lines of communication. A lean culture creates it.
